Classic napa cabbage kimchi — a tangy, spicy fermented side that gets better with time. Makes about 1 large jar.

This makes a full jar of traditional napa cabbage kimchi. The whole process takes a couple hours of active work plus a few days of fermenting on your counter before it heads to the fridge — taste along the way to catch your preferred level of tang.

Ingredients

  • 1 napa cabbage, medium head, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 0.3 cups coarse sea salt
  • 4 cups cups water
  • 1 medium carrot, julienned
  • 4 scallions, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoons ginger, grated
  • 3 tablespoons gochugaru Korean chili flakes
  • 1 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons rice flour
  • 0.5 cups cup water for rice paste

Steps

  1. Brine the cabbage: In a large bowl, dissolve 0.3 cups coarse sea salt in 4 cups cups water. Submerge the cabbage pieces in the brine, using a plate to weigh them down if needed. Let soak, tossing occasionally.
  2. Rinse and drain: Rinse the cabbage thoroughly under cold water 2-3 times to remove excess salt, then drain well in a colander.
  3. Make rice paste: In a small saucepan, whisk 2 tablespoons rice flour into 0.5 cups cup water for rice paste and heat over medium, stirring constantly, until it thickens into a loose paste, about 4 minutes
  4. Make the spice paste: In a large mixing bowl, combine the cooled rice paste with 5 garlic cloves, minced, 1 tablespoons ginger, grated, 3 tablespoons gochugaru Korean chili flakes, and 1 tablespoons sugar. Stir into a smooth, thick paste.
  5. Mix everything together: Add the drained cabbage, 1 medium carrot, julienned, and 4 scallions, cut into 1-inch pieces to the bowl with the spice paste. Wearing gloves, massage the paste into every leaf until fully coated.
  6. Pack into jar: Pack the kimchi tightly into a clean jar, pressing down so the brine rises above the vegetables. Leave about 2 inches of headspace, and seal loosely (or use a fermentation lid) to let gases escape.
  7. Ferment: Leave the jar at room temperature, out of direct sunlight. Press the cabbage down daily to keep it submerged. Taste starting on day 1 — it's ready when it reaches the tanginess you like.
  8. Refrigerate: Once fermented to your taste, seal tightly and move to the refrigerator. It keeps for months, growing more sour over time.
